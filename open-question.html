← T≡M Theory — Time Is Motion

The open question

Research note · the testable core, in half a page · Sep 2026 (rev. 3)

The claim

T≡M holds that time is identical to motion, with cosmological expansion as the root tick. Version 2.2 adds the condition that gives the identity its content: only a motion that closes on itself constitutes proper time (postulate T≡M-4). Its first consequence — no closure, no proper time — corresponds to the zero proper time along null trajectories in special relativity.

The weak, testable branch is a differential drift ε_R between clock species. A universal shift cancels in frequency ratios, and a constant per-species offset is absorbed into calibration, so only a drift that differs between clock types can be seen. Existing optical-clock comparisons bound it today at |Δε_R| ≲ 10⁻⁸.

ε_R should not be read as expansion “stretching” clocks. A bound system does not undergo a continuous stretching proportional to H: its response is all-or-nothing after transients (Price & Romano 2012), the known local effect of a cosmological constant is a static offset (Carrera & Giulini 2010), and the conclusion holds under quantum treatment as well (Lemos 2014). ε_R is the parameter of a residual coupling of unspecified origin.

The problem

Operationally, a differential drift is the same class of signal as a temporal variation of fundamental constants. A detection could not be attributed to T≡M without a rule for how ε_R depends on the type of transition — one that differs from the ordering by sensitivity coefficients to α, μ, and the rest.

The observable has the two-factor form that is standard in the phenomenology of locally coupled light scalars: a universal cosmological rate times a per-species coefficient (Trivedi 2025). In that literature the per-species coefficient has no independent experimental determination and is estimated from sensitivity to constants. That is the gap where a rule of its own would have to live.

What was tried

The closure condition suggests such a rule: systems with different types of closure might couple differently to the root tick. Five orderings were explored — closure compactness, binding strength, hierarchical level, rhythm affinity, and autonomy (the fraction of what sustains a closure that comes from its own internal relations). Autonomy went furthest: it is the only one whose direction follows from the framework rather than being chosen.

None of the five could be formalized as a coupling capable of entering the calculation of a frequency. They describe a structure; none says which physical object corresponds to it.

The linking hypothesis was therefore withdrawn. This is a result about the present formalism, not a proof of impossibility. The full report is in the Companion Paper v1.2.

The question

Is there a physically coherent way for a differential drift between clock species to be ordered by a structural property of the transition, rather than by sensitivity coefficients to fundamental constants — and if so, would that ordering be observationally distinguishable?

A related, more concrete one: the relation between clock drift and the evolving background is symmetric. A per-species coefficient different from the constants-sensitivity estimate would also bound the dark-energy equation of state differently, so clocks and cosmology would test the same hypothesis from two sides.

What is missing

A way to turn a relational property — closure, autonomy — into a physical object that enters the dynamics. Until that exists, T≡M remains an ontological framework with a bounded parameter and no signature of its own, and it says so. Every version, including this negative result, is archived with a DOI — see the revision history.
